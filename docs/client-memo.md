# Memo: Back-catalogue licensing, franchises or standalones?

**To:** Kwame Boateng, VP Content Acquisition · **Re:** $14M licensing review, 31 October
**Data as of:** 20 November 2026, public box office and budget figures for the 112 usable titles on this year's candidate list

## Recommendation
**Put the larger share of the $14M into franchise bundles, but only bundles you have checked in full.** Franchise films on this list earn more per dollar of budget and fail less often, in both periods and under every way I cleaned the data. The lead is smaller for recent films.

| Box office per $1 of budget, typical film | Franchise | Standalone |
|---|---|---|
| All years | **$5.60** (96 films) | $3.80 (16 films) |
| Released 2000 onward | **$5.10** (63 films) | $3.80 (8 films) |
| Films earning under $2 per $1 (all years) | 1 in 50 | 1 in 4 |

The answer does not flip. Older figures seem to include re-releases, so I lead with 2000 onward.

## Shortlist
**Franchises** (each one's weakest film beats the typical franchise film):
1. ***The Lord of the Rings***: $11.70, complete on the list.
2. ***Ice Age***: $8.30, or $7.50 with its 3 missing films; a new film is due January 2027.
3. ***The Avengers***: $6.90, complete; new films due December 2026 and 2027, so agree whether they join the bundle.

**Standalones** (all rated 8+ out of 10):
1. *Spirited Away* ($14.50): the best return of any film since 2000.
2. *Inception* ($5.20): beats the typical franchise film.
3. *Interstellar* ($4.50): the most-rated film on the list, so the widest audience.
4. *Memento* ($4.50): a strong return on the smallest budget ($9M).
5. *The Departed* ($3.20): the weakest; only 8 recent standalones were on offer.

*Second tier, if Finance accepts older figures:* *Pulp Fiction*, *Forrest Gump*, *Schindler's List*, *Back to the Future*.

## Talent as a lever
Talent mostly travels with the franchise: use it to negotiate, not as proof. **Strong** (3+ films since 2000, above typical): director Peter Jackson; leads Elijah Wood, Ray Romano, Daniel Radcliffe, Robert Downey Jr. **Below typical:** Johnny Depp, Christian Bale, George Clooney. **Christopher Nolan** has the best-rated films of any director but below-typical returns: a quality argument, not a money one.

## Deal note: *The Lord of the Rings*
Complete, made after 2000 and rated 8.5/10. A typical film earned $11.70 per $1, 2.3 times the typical post-2000 franchise film ($5.10). **I would pay up to about twice the per-film price of a typical franchise title, and walk away above 2.3 times.** The number that drove this view: its *weakest* film, *The Fellowship of the Ring*, earned $9.40 per $1, more than the typical film of every other franchise made since 2000.

## The five-film bundle you passed on
Six franchises on the list have exactly five films. Counted whole, the typical one returned **$3.65 per $1**, below a typical recent standalone; only *Toy Story* ($5.60) beat the typical recent franchise film. **Unless that bundle was as strong, passing was more likely a bullet dodged than a miss.** The same whole-bundle check on it would settle it.

## What would change my view
- **Price:** recent franchise films earn about 30% more per dollar than standalones; a bundle priced more than 30% higher per film loses that edge.
- **Missing films** can nearly halve a franchise's figure (below).
- **StreamVault's own viewing figures**, if they rank these titles differently.

## What I could not stand behind
- **Most bundles are only partly on the list:** 19 of 32 franchises miss released films, often weak sequels. Counted in full, *Star Wars* falls from $10.80 to $6.20, *Jurassic Park* from $8.90 to $5.00. **Get the full film list before pricing any bundle.**
- **Older revenue probably includes re-releases**, which I cannot separate.
- **Only 8 recent standalones:** two films either way moves their figure by a fifth.
- **Box office is not streaming demand,** budgets exclude marketing, and every title is a well-known hit, so these figures rank *these* candidates rather than forecast viewing.
- **15 of 127 list entries were unusable** (7 repeats, 5 numbers matching no film, 3 blank or junk).
